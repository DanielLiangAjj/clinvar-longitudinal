Interactive analysis of ClinVar reclassification, 2012–2025

A longitudinal reprocessing of 3,678,303 variants across 148 monthly ClinVar snapshots, distinguishing genuine clinical reinterpretation from database/schema evolution. Every panel below is interactive — hover for values, drag to zoom, click legend entries to toggle series.

Total unique variants in ClinVar over time, by record format era.

Growth accelerated sharply after the 2020 transition to variant-centric (VCV) records.

2 · Classification landscape

Proportional distribution of the nine normalized classification categories across snapshots.

VUS is the dominant and growing category; toggle series in the legend to isolate trends.

3 · Reclassification, stratified by clinical significance

The headline "9.3% of variants reclassified" decomposed by the most clinically significant change each variant underwent. Only a small fraction crosses the clinical actionability boundary.

Left: variants by most-severe change (% of all 3.68M). Right: all reclassification events by conflict severity. Adjacent within-tier moves (P↔LP, B↔LB) are treated as concordant by ClinVar.

4 · Time-to-event (censored survival)

Cumulative incidence of a variant's first reclassification, estimated by Kaplan–Meier over all variants with right-censoring — not only among those that were reclassified.

Left: overall cumulative incidence with 95% CI (median never reached — under half of variants are ever reclassified). Right: 5- and 10-year cumulative incidence by disease domain.

5 · Reclassification mechanisms

Attribution of 697,698 reclassification events to observable mechanisms, and their shift across schema eras.

Reinterpretation dominated the RCV era; inter-submitter conflict emergence dominates the multi-submitter VCV era.

6 · Effect sizes & confounder-adjusted odds

Group comparisons with risk ratios (95% CI), and a multivariable logistic regression adjusting for baseline submissions, review status, follow-up, era, variant class and gene size.

Left: unadjusted risk ratios (log axis). Right: adjusted odds ratios — the disease-domain and cancer effects are attenuated but survive adjustment.

7 · Interrupted time-series

Monthly trends around two interventions: the 2015 ACMG/AMP guidelines and the 2020 VCV transition. Segmented regressions detect level/slope changes.

8 · Disease-domain variability

Reclassification rate and evidence depth across curated clinical domains.

Hereditary-cancer genes reclassify roughly twice as often as the database average.

Left: most-cited PMIDs (share of cited variants) — SHERLOC and the 2015 ACMG/AMP guideline dwarf all variant-specific references. Right: mean references per variant, raw vs. method-filtered.

Data & methods

Laboratory anonymization. All submitter identities are replaced with coded labels (Laboratory A, B, C…) by submission rank, consistent with the manuscript. No real laboratory name appears in this site or the published data.

Source data are the publicly available NCBI ClinVar monthly XML releases. The multi-GB raw snapshots and per-variant tables are not redistributed here (regenerable via the pipeline); this repository contains the analysis code, aggregate result files, figures, and this interactive companion. See the repository README for full reproduction instructions and the Zenodo archive DOI.

Analyses: era-stratified reclassification detection, clinical-severity stratification, right-censored Kaplan–Meier time-to-event, χ²/risk-ratio comparisons, multivariable logistic regression with baseline covariates, segmented interrupted time-series, and guideline-citation filtering.
